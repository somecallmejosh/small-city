# Small City Studios — business discovery and website strategy

Working draft · November 8, 2026

## Status and purpose

The project now explicitly includes guidance to help Chase make the business sustainable, alongside the website work. Start with the [owner roadmap](./owner-roadmap.md) for the broader mandate, actions, responsibilities, and review routine. This document supports that work with discovery and website strategy.

It combines the owner's goals supplied in the project brief, a review of the public website, and a small comparison of local studio offers. Recommendations are hypotheses until supported by owner interviews, customer research, and business data. No customer interviews, analytics audit, search ranking verification, mobile usability study, or performance audit has been completed. The local project folder was empty at the start of this work.

The business objective is to increase sustainable income from mixing and engineering, including remote projects, while retaining a focus on rap recording and beat production. The website should help suitable artists understand the work, hear evidence, choose an appropriate service, and commit to a clearly scoped project. The user's follow-up establishes mixing and engineering as the lead offer, superseding the initial production-led hypothesis.

## 2. The central strategic hypothesis

Shift the purchase from access to a room toward Chase's mixing and engineering contribution: capturing a strong performance and finishing an existing recording, with scope and pricing appropriate to the artist's actual needs. Beat production remains a distinct supporting service.

An expensive-looking website cannot create purchasing power in the existing audience. Better-fit work may also require different acquisition channels, credited mixing examples, referrals, and a remote mixing workflow. Remote work is a confirmed business direction; operational readiness is still to be established. It removes dependence on the artist attending the room, but does not automatically create higher budgets or less admin.

Working internal positioning:

> Small City Studios helps independent rap artists record and finish their music with mixing and engineering by Chase Briley, alongside beat production.

This is an offer direction, not a verified claim about results. Prove the particular value through credited work and the process. Avoid promises about fame, chart performance, placements, or commercial success.

Define fit through behavior and scope: prepared material or a clear need for development, realistic timing, an agreed budget, willingness to collaborate, and respect for the session. Age, income, follower count, and career status are poor substitutes for these criteria. A first-time artist can be an excellent client.

## 3. Discovery to complete before setting prices or designing polished screens

### Owner and economics interview

Advertised prices, informal actual pricing, service priority, estimated mix duration, overhead, and owner income goal are now established above. Next define a repeatable scope and quoting method for stereo-beat vocal mixing versus separate-track mixing, establish realistic weekly capacity and total effort, and confirm exact credits for a small portfolio selection. Use estimates from recent real songs when records do not exist; label estimates explicitly.

Then establish:

1. What would an ideal week look like: recording, beat creation, mixing, admin, and days off?
2. Which recent projects were rewarding and profitable? Which consumed unbilled time, and why?
3. Reconstruct up to three recent jobs from memory or existing payment/calendar records if readily available; do not make a historical reconstruction project a prerequisite. Start logging new inquiries and all work time going forward.
4. What are monthly fixed costs, direct project costs, realistic capacity, and current cancellation or no-show losses?
5. What does production mean in Chase's offer: an existing beat license, an exclusive beat, a custom composition, arrangement, vocal direction, or full-song production?
6. What rights, deliverables, revisions, payment schedule, and turnaround does each service currently include?
7. Which session boundaries can Chase consistently enforce? How many non-performing guests can the room support?
8. Where do good clients originate, why do they return, and what do they tell friends about Chase?
9. What is needed to deliver remote mixing reliably: file intake, source review, feedback, payment, revisions, and delivery? Remote work is desired; readiness needs confirmation.
10. Which address, phone number, biography, credits, photographs, and public claims are accurate today?

### Customer evidence

Invite, with the user's authorization, a small initial set of past and prospective customers: approximately three strong repeat clients, two recent first-time clients, and two inquiries that did not book. This is directional qualitative research, not a representative survey. Do not send invitations without authorization.

Ask about the last real purchase: what triggered the search, alternatives considered, examples they listened to, what was unclear, expected total cost, how they judged trust, why they booked or stopped, and what happened after the session. Avoid asking whether they like a proposed design or would hypothetically pay more.

### Analytics and sales baseline

First establish whether analytics/Search Console exist. Where available, review a recent 90-day period and the prior comparable period. Check tracking coverage before interpreting conversion rates. Missing business records should be addressed with a prospective baseline: log new inquiries and completed jobs for an initial 30 days, extend if volume or sales-cycle length is insufficient, and avoid claiming statistical significance from a small sample. Design can advance with clearly labeled assumptions while this evidence accumulates.

- Search Console: queries, landing pages, geography where available, clicks, impressions, and changes over time. Separate brand and non-brand demand.
- Website analytics: organic landing pages, device mix, service views, contact actions, form attempts/errors/successes, and existing acquisition attribution.
- Booking records: inquiry source, service, quoted value, deposits, completed projects, cancellations, repeat work, and time spent.
- Manual call/text attribution where practical: ask how the artist found the studio. A click on a phone link does not prove a conversation or sale.
- Trace the existing form's delivery and Chase's follow-up process using a clearly identified test submission only when operational testing is authorized.

Output: an evidence-backed problem statement, a service-level profitability view, a desired service mix, and an agreed definition of a qualified inquiry.

## 4. Offer architecture to validate

| Offer | Artist's need | Candidate scope | Sales route |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Mixing and finishing | Improve an existing recording | File review, defined mix scope, agreed revision allowance and delivery formats; mastering only if included | Service inquiry with a private demo link; files after scope agreement |
| Recording with an engineer | Capture prepared material | Booked session block, engineering, clear distinction between a reference bounce and finished mix | Visible rate/minimum, preparation checklist, request a session |
| Beat production with Chase | Find or develop a beat for the artist's song | Distinguish an existing beat license from custom work; agree deliverables and rights | Brief inquiry, examples, scope and terms |

Lead with mixing and engineering, including a dedicated remote route once its workflow is ready. Keep recording easy to find for local organic intent and beat production visible as a supporting service. Remove listening parties from primary navigation and the main offer comparison; retaining them as a separate offering is an owner/economics decision. Do not add a beat marketplace, memberships, or recurring retainers until there is evidence they solve a real buying need.

Publish an honest minimum or starting price once the economics are known. Explain what it buys, common extras, and why a project may cost more. A low anchor that excludes essential work would recreate the current confusion.

Separate existing beat licenses from custom production. Before publishing, document the offered rights, exclusivity, stems, credits, permitted uses, and any royalty or split arrangements. Do not imply that a library beat is exclusive or custom-made for an artist unless that is true.

### Pricing method

Calculate an operating revenue requirement from owner compensation, operating costs, appropriate reserves, and any employment or tax costs applicable to the business. Divide it by realistic billable capacity, allowing time for unpaid admin, marketing, downtime, and leave. Confirm the treatment of these costs with the owner's accounting process.

For each service, estimate all delivery time: intake, preparation, production, session work, editing, revisions, exports, delivery, and associated admin. Add direct project expenses and use that scope to test whether the price supports the revenue requirement. Model realistic utilization rather than assuming every available hour sells.

Track realized owner return per total work hour alongside total monthly income. At the advertised $100 price and estimated one-to-two-hour duration, illustrative gross revenue per job hour is $50–$100 before expenses. Actual pricing is informal, so this is a diagnostic range, not a measured realized rate. Extra revisions and unpaid business time can change it. At 48 working weeks, the supplied income/overhead figures require $2,125 in collected revenue per working week, before unreported costs and taxes.

Local reference points reviewed on November 8, 2026: [Human Ipod Studios](https://humanipodstudios.com/booking/) publishes $60–$75 hourly studio rates, a two-hour minimum, and a deposit policy; [The Nation Studios](https://www.thenationstudios.com/book-online) publishes a $120 two-hour recording option. These are public asking prices, not evidence of occupancy, profitability, comparable scope, or what Small City should charge. Competing on the same hourly menu makes differentiation harder; Chase needs specific proof of his contribution.

## 5. Session experience and boundaries

The website can prepare expectations, but booking communication and in-studio practice must uphold them.

Candidate policy copy, subject to Chase's decision:

> Keep your session focused. Bring the people contributing to the record, and confirm any additional guests before your visit.

If Chase sets a numerical guest limit, state it plainly before payment, in the confirmation, and in the reminder. Exceptions should be agreed in advance. Separate planned collaborators from non-performing guests in the intake or confirmation. Do not describe clients or their friends as a nuisance in public copy.

Also define arrival and late-time rules, minimum booking, overtime authorization, deposit and cancellation terms, revision limits, file retention/delivery, and the difference between session output and a completed mix. Show material terms before commitment. The deposit should reserve agreed work; an inquiry alone should never imply a confirmed session.

Send a short preparation checklist: lyrics and references, suitable beat/stem files, permissions for supplied material, session goal, participating artists, and arrival details. Let an unprepared artist choose a production/development service rather than failing an assumed readiness test.

## 6. Proposed information architecture

Preserve existing useful URLs where possible. This is a provisional architecture, pending landing-page and query evidence.

| Page | Main buying question | Required content |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Home `/` | Is this the right collaborator for my music? | Clear specialty/location, Chase, relevant audio, service choices, proof, next step |
| Mixing `/mixing/` | Can he improve the recordings I have? | Relevant credited examples, remote process, file requirements, revision/delivery scope, investment |
| Recording `/recording/` | What happens in a session and what does it cost? | Engineer contribution, minimum, included output, preparation, guests, location |
| Beat production `/production/` | Can Chase help develop my beat? | Credited beat examples, collaboration process, scope, rights overview, starting investment |
| Work `/projects/` | Do I like the sound, and what did Chase do? | Curated playable work, accurate roles, genre/context, relevant service links |
| Meet Chase `/bio/` | Who will I work with? | Real portrait, approach, current factual biography, specific experience and work |
| Start a project `/booking/` | What is the next step? | Service-specific request, price context, contact method, response expectations |
| Contact `/contact/` | How do I reach or find the studio? | Consistent contact/location, directions, simple alternative inquiry route |

### Homepage sequence

1. A clear rap recording and mixing headline, beat production in supporting copy, verified location, and human introduction. Main action: start a project, with immediate choices for remote mixing and studio recording. Secondary action: hear relevant work.
2. Three concise audio examples with exact Chase credits and obvious playback controls.
3. Service choices described through artist needs: mixing first, studio recording second, beat production third.
4. A real project story explaining the brief, Chase's contribution, and the delivered work. Use verified facts rather than invented performance statistics.
5. Chase's working approach and a real portrait or studio session photograph.
6. The inquiry → scope → deposit → session/production → delivery process.
7. Transparent investment context and focused FAQs, including first sessions, supplied beats, collaborators, and remote work if available.
8. A final project action with a credible description of what happens next.

### Inquiry flow

Organic landing page → relevant work and service → scope/price expectations → short request → confirmation → human review → agreed scope and deposit → preparation → delivery → repeat project.

Use one short screen unless research shows progressive steps help. Required fields should have a clear use: artist/name, service, brief goal, and one reliable contact channel. Budget bands can help route projects once real minimums exist; include an unsure option with price guidance. Timing and an optional private demo link can add context. Ask detailed production and guest questions after the initial request or conditionally, avoiding a long mandatory application.

Service-specific links should preselect the service without hiding the ability to change it. Preserve entries after validation errors, show errors beside fields, support keyboard/screen-reader use, and prevent duplicate submission. Confirm receipt only after server success. State that availability and scope are pending confirmation. Publish a response window Chase can reliably meet, with an alternative contact route if it passes.

Track qualified inquiries through paid deposits and completed projects. Phone/text routes remain available and need an internal logging process so the website form is not mistaken for the whole funnel.

## 7. Design brief

The visual direction should communicate a working rap recording and mixing studio with a capable person behind the sound. Aim for confident, direct, and welcoming. Avoid imitating luxury branding as a proxy for client seriousness or using language that polices who counts as a real artist.

Provisional direction: editorial typography, a restrained palette derived from the actual studio, ample space around work samples, and authentic photography of Chase producing, directing a vocal session, and listening with an artist. Color, typefaces, and light/dark treatment remain design decisions to explore after discovery.

Keep genre, service, location, and the next action clear on mobile. Use legible body text, visible focus states, sufficient contrast, comfortably sized controls, and a logical reading order. Audio must be user-initiated, keyboard operable, and labeled by track and credit. Play only one preview at a time. Use short, permission-cleared excerpts; provide explicit-content information where appropriate. Any before/after examples need consent, matching passages, and comparable loudness to avoid misleading comparisons.

Do not put critical evidence behind a required Spotify login or an immediate trip to another platform. Prefer lightweight first-party previews where rights permit; load third-party players on interaction. No autoplay audio or decorative animation that interferes with listening, reading, or completing the inquiry.

Deliverables after discovery: a written content hierarchy, mobile-first wireframes for home/service/inquiry, two visual directions built around the same offer, a chosen responsive system, and a prototype covering the actual inquiry states. Test with representative artists using real tasks before development.

- Two or three concise project stories: starting point, artist intent, Chase's work, final deliverables, and a relevant artist quote where available.

## 9. Search preservation and implementation requirements

Strong search visibility is a valuable input, but it does not establish that the current traffic matches the desired service or geography. Use query and landing-page evidence before changing page intent.

- Inventory indexed URLs, important inbound links, titles, copy, canonical tags, and current service/location relevance.
- Keep useful URLs. If a URL must change, map its direct permanent redirect and update internal links.
- Correct location information truthfully across website and business listings; do not preserve an inaccurate location phrase for rankings.
- Build substantive service pages around actual demand and work; avoid interchangeable city pages or generic content volume.
- Preserve relevant genre and service language, accurate structured data, crawlable copy, an XML sitemap, and correct canonical/indexing behavior.
- Keep staging out of search; verify that production can be indexed at launch.
- Use semantic, server-rendered pages and minimal client JavaScript. Astro is a plausible fit for a content-led site, but the stack is not yet selected.
- Confirm how inquiry processing, spam protection, error handling, delivery, analytics, and booking records will work before choosing integrations.
- Validate performance on representative mobile devices and actual media. Do not claim a performance improvement before measuring it.
- Test forms, contact links, keyboard use, media controls, responsive layouts, redirects, and data capture before launch.

## 10. Measurement and experiment plan

Primary business outcome: sustainable owner income from the desired service mix. Website conversion supports that objective; raw lead count alone does not prove success.

| Measure | Definition / purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Qualified inquiry | Meets an agreed service, scope/budget, timing, and working-expectation fit; new artists can qualify |
| Organic qualified-inquiry rate | Qualified inquiries attributed to organic ÷ organic visits, using a consistent denominator and explicit attribution limits |
| Inquiry-to-deposit rate | Paid projects ÷ inquiries from a defined inquiry cohort; allow for the booking lag |
| Collected project value | Payments actually collected, not calculator totals or unsigned quotes |
| Return per total work hour | Collected revenue less allocated project/operating costs, divided by all relevant work time; document the cost allocation method |
| Mixing/engineering share | Revenue and hours from mixing and engineering, with remote/local work distinguished; beat production tracked separately |
| Response time, cancellations, repeat work | Shows whether the operational experience supports conversion and retention |

Candidate events: service viewed, audio started, project request started, validation error, request successfully received, phone/text action, qualified inquiry, proposal issued, deposit paid, project completed. Keep project audio, contact details, and free-text messages out of analytics event payloads. Link sales outcomes internally with an inquiry identifier where practical. A CTA click is a micro-conversion, not a booking.

Establish a baseline and use owner capacity/economics to set numerical targets. Do not invent an uplift target now. At low volume, prioritize qualitative task testing and cohort review; an A/B test may take too long to resolve. Compare traffic mix, seasonality, response behavior, and booking lag before attributing changes to design.

First hypotheses to test: credited audio improves confidence; clear scope/pricing improves fit; a short contextual inquiry reduces back-and-forth; preparation and guest expectations reduce disruption. Evaluate both conversion and owner workload so tighter qualification does not hide a fall in viable business.
