Working draft · November 8, 2026 · Internal planning, not a public rate card.

This model supports the broader [owner roadmap](./owner-roadmap.md), which turns the financial and service decisions into actions for Chase, client acquisition tests, delivery practices, and a recurring business review.

- Revisions: no fixed cap. The initial explanation mentioned hourly charges, but subsequent clarification establishes that pricing is informal. There is no verified uniform mixing rate or revision charge.

## What the current mix price implies

If every mix actually collects $100, a mix-only model requires 1,020 songs/year. Over 48 working weeks, that is 21.25 songs per week on average. At one to two hours each, delivery alone takes approximately 21–43 hours/week, with any omitted admin/sales time on top.

That model may be feasible at the faster end with reliable demand and scope control; it is much tighter at the slower end. Neither workload nor demand is established. A more valuable mix offer might reduce required project volume, but customers' willingness to buy it must be tested. Do not choose a premium price solely by dividing the owner's income goal.

The discovery finding is not that $100 is automatically wrong. It is that informal pricing and unmeasured effort make it difficult to plan capacity or explain the purchase consistently.

## Proposed service structure for review

These are proposed scope rules, not approved customer terms. Prices, turnaround, payment, exact inclusions, and file specifications remain to be set.

| Service | Base scope to define | Scope that requires explicit assessment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Vocal mix over a stereo beat | Supplied stereo instrumental and organized vocal recordings; vocal balance/processing and agreed finishing/delivery | Extensive comping/timing/tuning, damaged sources, alternate arrangements, extra artists/versions, missing files |
| Separate-track mix | Organized instrumental and vocal tracks; mix and agreed finishing/delivery | Track complexity, substantial editing or production, restoration, changes to source material after work starts |
| Recording and engineering | A confirmed session block with agreed participating artists and session output | Overtime, preparation/production outside the block, separately delivered final mixing/mastering |
| Beat production | A clearly identified license or custom creation with agreed files and rights | Exclusive rights, stems, substantial custom development, revisions and changes to brief |

Public service navigation can remain simple: Mixing, Recording, Beats. Within Mixing, use plain-language choices such as "vocals + a finished beat" and "separate instrument + vocal tracks," with an unsure option. Scope categories must not imply that one customer receives a deliberately inferior mix.

Recommended quoting method:

1. For mixing, review a rough/demo and a brief source description before confirming scope and price. Ask for detailed files only when needed to assess or begin the work.
2. Confirm one project fee covering a defined scope, or explicitly agree an hourly basis with an estimate and approval point. Avoid an ambiguous combination of both.
3. Proposed fixed-fee revision allowance: two rounds of consolidated feedback within the original brief. This is a candidate operating policy for Chase to evaluate, not a settled promise.
4. Explain that new recordings, a changed creative direction, or extra work may change the quote. Agree additional charges before performing that work. Chase's own corrections should not consume the artist's paid revision allowance.
5. Confirm delivery formats, turnaround starting conditions, payment timing, and file retention before the artist commits. Avoid an unlimited-revisions promise or an arbitrary speed guarantee.
6. Offer recording as a clearly bounded session with an agreed overrun policy and an explicit distinction between a reference bounce and a final delivered mix.

The fee for each scope should reflect actual effort and value, then be tested against completed-project economics and artist understanding. Keep the published site and actual quotes aligned. Honest scope-based starting prices are useful once their inclusions are defined.

## UX consequences

- Lead the homepage with rap recording and mixing; keep beat production in the opening service description and service navigation.
- Give remote mixing a direct route independent of studio location and room-booking language.
- On the mixing inquiry, ask whether the artist has a stereo beat or separate tracks, including an unsure option. Do not ask artists to know their own mixing time.
- Replace the generic quantity calculator with relevant scope guidance and a project request. A calculator could return later only if the underlying pricing rules become reliable.
- Show service inclusions, price basis, revision expectations, and the quote process before the artist contacts Chase. Keep detailed export guidance for the appropriate handoff stage.
- Show accurate mixing credits beside audio. Hearing a strong track is useful; knowing what Chase did makes it relevant evidence for this offer.
- Keep a welcoming recording route for newcomers, while setting preparation, guest, and payment expectations before booking.

## How to validate the model without reconstructing years of records

For the next ten completed projects, record agreed scope/price, amount collected, time by broad activity, revision rounds, and whether actual work exceeded the quote. Ten is a manageable initial operational sample, not a statistically representative dataset. Include a mix of stereo-beat vocal jobs, separate-track work, and recording where those jobs occur naturally; do not invent balanced demand.

Alongside this, log new inquiries and their outcomes. Note why a quote was declined only when known; silence does not establish price as the cause. Review weekly and compare collected revenue with the baseline requirement. Extend the observation period for volume and booking lag.

The desired website result is more adequately priced, suitable mixing/engineering work that supports the target income within a workable schedule. Higher traffic, longer visits, and more form submissions are supporting measures, not substitutes for that outcome.
