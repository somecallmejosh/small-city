## Two distinct customer journeys

### Remote mixing

1. Artist enters through a mixing page, a work example, a referral, or the homepage.
2. They hear a relevant example and see Chase's exact role.
3. They see scope, investment context, required files, process, and timing expectations.
4. They request a mix with a rough/demo link if available. Do not require a large multitrack upload to make first contact.
5. Chase checks fit and source quality, then confirms scope, price, payment terms, revisions, and delivery schedule.
6. After agreement, the artist receives one clear file-handoff method and naming/export guidance appropriate to their recording setup.
7. Chase delivers a review version; the artist submits consolidated notes through an agreed channel.
8. Agreed revisions and final delivery follow the stated payment/delivery arrangement.

Define secure file access, link expiration, backup/retention, payment, and feedback practices before launching this route. No requirement to build a custom customer portal at launch. Remote work expands reach and reduces dependence on in-room attendance, but sales effort, source problems, and revision time still need management.

### Local recording

1. Artist enters through a recording/local search page or the homepage.
2. They see recording examples, the actual studio/engineer, accurate location, rate, minimum, and included output.
3. They request a session with a goal and preferred timing; scheduling remains pending confirmation.
4. Chase confirms scope, availability, price, participating artists/guests, and booking/payment terms.
5. The artist receives preparation and arrival guidance.
6. Session and any separately agreed mixing/delivery work follow.

Avoid sending a local recording search visitor through a remote mixing application. Both paths should share the same visual system and simple inquiry infrastructure while asking relevant questions.

## A manageable evidence baseline

Begin with one inquiry/job log and Chase as its owner, using an existing tool he will actually maintain. Tool selection is deferred; no new subscription or public system is needed for discovery.

Minimum inquiry entry: date, service, source as reported/known, status, and next action. Include phone/text inquiries. Minimum paid-job entry: agreed price, amount collected, total work hours including revisions/admin, and completion date. Add remote/local and new/repeat labels when easy. Use an internal identifier; avoid copying private messages or audio into analytics.

Review weekly for approximately 15 minutes: unanswered inquiries, reasons work did not proceed, price versus actual effort, and whether mixing/engineering are increasing. Over the first 30 days, count inquiries, proposals, paid work, and actual delivery effort; allow cohorts to mature rather than treating pending requests as lost sales. Extend the observation window when volume is low.

If historical Search Console or analytics exist, use them. Their absence does not justify claiming that the new design improves conversion. A baseline collected during changes is an early operating benchmark with limited causal value, not a clean controlled comparison.
