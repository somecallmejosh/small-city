# Small City Studios — owner roadmap to a sustainable business

Working draft · November 8, 2026

## Project mandate

Develop a sustainable owner-operated rap recording, mixing, and beat-production business, led by mixing and engineering, including remote projects. Help Chase make and maintain the commercial and operational changes required to support full-time work. The website is one delivery stream within this broader engagement.

The outcome is a business that can consistently fund its operating costs, provide the intended owner income, fulfill commitments, and fit a workable schedule. A redesigned website or increased lead count is insufficient evidence of that outcome.

Use the current financial baseline of $102,000/year in collected revenue to cover reported overhead and the stated owner income goal. Update it for complete costs and the intended meaning of take-home income. See [business model](./business-model.md). Demand, weekly capacity, and actual profitability have not been established. This roadmap is a set of proposed actions and review criteria, not a guarantee of sustainability within 90 days.

## Owner readiness and candid assessment

The user reports that Chase avoids parts of business administration and is uncomfortable with self-marketing and rate increases. Provide a candid assessment of required outcomes and practical help carrying them out. Start with the [sustainability assessment and owner commitments](./sustainability-assessment.md), including an owner-facing discussion, essential responsibilities, manageable methods, and a proposed working agreement.

Separate required business functions from optional tactics; give every essential function an owner or workable process. Specific rate increases must follow economics and market evidence. If Chase's chosen prices, capacity, costs, or acquisition methods cannot support his goal, explain the shortfall and available choices.

## First working session: the week and business Chase wants

Proposed 60–90-minute facilitated session, using rough estimates where records are absent:

1. Map an ideal week: days/hours, recording, mixing, beats, admin, marketing, and time off. Identify which hours can realistically be sold.
2. Walk through three recent jobs: first contact, price agreed, actual work, revisions, payment, and next project. Identify friction and work Chase enjoys.
3. Complete the expense picture and clarify whether $1,500/week is before or after personal taxes. Identify current cash commitments and unpaid balances privately; no sensitive details need appear in public project materials.
4. Identify five examples of clients Chase wants more of and the actual reasons: fit, reliability, collaboration, project value, repeat work, or efficient delivery. Do not use age or follower count as a proxy.
5. Choose the service to standardize first and the smallest daily tracking routine he will maintain.
6. Discuss the sustainability assessment directly. Identify responsibilities Chase will perform, where he needs practice/support, and whether affordable help is available. Agree an initial 30-day routine and review point.

Output: a one-page owner brief, priority service, realistic capacity, updated income/revenue requirement, and responsibilities. Begin discovery and website content work from this brief; do not wait for perfect historical records.

## Days 1–30: establish a usable baseline and reduce avoidable friction

### Chase's actions

- Record new inquiries, current project stages, payments, and total work time. A familiar existing tool is preferable to an elaborate system he will abandon.
- Use a written scope and price basis for new projects. Confirm revisions, delivery, payment, scheduling, and changes to brief. Review the proposed service terms in the business model before using them.
- Confirm the correct phone destination, actual location, and who receives website inquiries.
- Choose three strong portfolio examples and confirm exact credits and permissions.
- Identify a small group of former good-fit clients and referral relationships for possible research and outreach. The agent will not contact anyone without explicit authorization.

### Project support

- Facilitate the owner session and help establish the log/review routine.
- Review any available analytics and Search Console; document what is missing.
- Interview a small, authorized customer sample about real buying decisions.
- Draft offer sheets and reply/quote/preparation templates.
- Map remote mixing and local recording journeys; begin content hierarchy and wireframes with open terms clearly marked.

### Review questions

Can Chase explain his priority offer, scope, price basis, and next step consistently? Do new inquiries get a recorded next action? Do we have usable early time/payment evidence? Which bottleneck deserves the next experiment? A low number of completed jobs means a longer measurement window, not invented certainty.

Exact volumes and spend depend on Chase's capacity and evidence; no budget or outreach has been authorized. Count owner marketing time when assessing the economics. Do not assume posting more often or buying traffic is automatically the answer.

Use actual inquiry questions and quote outcomes to improve copy, scope, and process. Record price objections only when expressed; an unanswered quote does not prove the price was too high. Avoid changing price, scope, channel, and follow-up simultaneously when trying to understand a result.

Website deliverables during this period: tested service content, mobile-first home/service/inquiry wireframes, visual directions, and a responsive prototype. Operational tests can use accurate existing pages while the new site develops.

## Days 61–90: implement, review, and decide what to continue

- Implement and launch the website when core offers, inquiry ownership, delivery workflow, and launch checks are ready. The dates are a planning frame, not a deadline that overrides readiness.
- Review completed-project economics by service and complexity, including revision/admin time.
- Continue acquisition activities that produce suitable paid work at a tolerable cost in money and time; revise or stop ineffective activities based on enough evidence.
- Use agreed session and revision boundaries consistently. Discuss exceptions before work begins and log their effect.
- Add a light completion routine: confirm delivery, ask what is next for the artist, and request feedback or a referral when appropriate. Do not require a testimonial or offer an unplanned discount to earn a repeat booking.
- Review the cash outlook, upcoming commitments, workload, and actual owner income. Decide whether the evidence supports the next stage of full-time operation or calls for another test period.

Output: an updated business model, maintained operating routine, implemented sales tools, and an evidence-based next-quarter plan. Ninety days may reveal which model is viable; it may not establish dependable demand or seasonality.

## The operating routine

**During the working day:** log new inquiries and their next actions; record time as work is completed; update payment and delivery status. Aim for a few minutes of upkeep rather than a reconstruction task at month-end.

**Weekly, approximately 30 minutes:** Chase reviews the next two weeks of work and cash commitments, unanswered inquiries/quotes, completed-job effort, acquisition actions, and one improvement for the coming week. If the log is too burdensome, simplify it before adding automation.

**Monthly:** reconcile bookkeeping and review the complete cost picture, owner income, service mix, collected revenue, and forward cash outlook. Use bookkeeping/accounting support for financial records and tax treatment as needed; the website dashboard is not the accounting record.

Practical weekly review template:

| Question | Entry |
| --- | --- |
| What did we collect, what costs/commitments are due, and what was available for owner income? | Actual amounts; distinguish deposits for work still to be delivered |
| Which new inquiries fit the service, scope, budget, and timing? | Count, source, next action |
| Which projects were agreed/paid, and what remains pending? | Project IDs, value, stage; no double counting deposits and balances |
| How much work did completed jobs really take? | Price, total hours, revision effort, service type |
| What work is already agreed for the next two weeks? | Delivery hours and expected payment timing; separate tentative requests |
| What produced repeat or referred work, and what will we do next week? | Known source, specific action, person responsible |

Keep bookkeeping and forecast records private. Measure inquiries and jobs at consistent stages; allow time for inquiries to become bookings. Early figures are operational learning, not statistically reliable conversion benchmarks.

## A repeatable quote and delivery checklist

For each new project, record:

- Artist, service, project goal, and source-file situation.
- Included work and outputs; excluded/optional work described plainly.
- Fixed fee or hourly basis, estimate, payment schedule, and approval for extras.
- Revision/feedback process and how a changed brief is handled.
- Start conditions, agreed timeline, booking/guest terms where relevant.
- File handoff, delivery method, and retention expectations.
- One next action, who owns it, and when it will happen.

At completion, capture collected value, total effort, any exception, and a relevant next-project opportunity. Asking about the artist's plans should help them plan the work; recurring packages or subscriptions require demonstrated recurring need and workable capacity before being introduced.

## Decisions when results differ from the plan

| Observed problem | Investigate first | Candidate response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Too few suitable inquiries | Reach, audience, proof, service relevance, referral activity | Improve a specific acquisition route or offer explanation |
| Inquiries but few agreed projects | Fit, investment clarity, response speed, source readiness, known objections | Improve qualification/quote/follow-up; validate scope and price |
| Enough jobs, weak owner income | Total effort, underquoting, discounts, revisions, costs | Adjust scope/fees or delivery method; reduce avoidable unpaid work |
| Healthy quotes, insufficient available cash | Payment timing, overdue balances, delivery obligations, costs/draws | Update payment/collection practices and cash forecast |
| Good initial sales, little repeat work | Client experience, release cadence, fit, delivery consistency | Improve completion and relevant follow-up; investigate real reasons |
| Revenue on target, unsustainable schedule | Capacity, admin load, context switching, service mix | Rebalance workload and pricing; evaluate affordable support |

Every diagnosis needs evidence. Do not label all objections as client seriousness, all lost quotes as price problems, or all low sales as website failures.

## Sustainability review

Assess a sustained period appropriate to project volume and seasonality, rather than one unusually strong week. Look for owner income and costs being funded, sufficient cash for near-term commitments, realistic forward demand, profitable delivery at actual effort, a workable schedule, and regular client acquisition/retention activity. Agree specific numerical thresholds with Chase after complete costs and capacity are established.

If the business cannot meet those conditions, revisit service mix, market, prices, cost base, or the operating model. A larger site or more marketing spend should be justified by a diagnosed constraint.
