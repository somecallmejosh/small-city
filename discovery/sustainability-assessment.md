# Small City Studios — sustainability assessment and owner commitments

Working draft · November 8, 2026 · For discussion with Chase; no communication has been sent to him.

## Purpose

The user reports that Chase is uncomfortable with self-marketing, rate increases, and business administration. Treat this as a constraint for the operating plan, not a diagnosis or a criticism of character. Explore what makes each activity difficult instead of assuming the cause.

State commercial requirements and the consequences of leaving them unmet plainly. Provide practical help with follow-through. Distinguish necessary outcomes from optional methods: adequate project economics and sufficient demand are essential; a particular price or social-media schedule is not.

## Owner-facing opening for the working session

Chase, your goal is full-time studio work with $1,500 a week available after business expenses. Based on the costs supplied, that requires about $8,500 a month in revenue, before taxes or any costs we have not included.

The business needs enough paying work, prices and scope that cover actual effort, reliable payment and delivery, and a way to see whether those things are happening. The website can help people discover your work and buy with confidence. You also need a workable process for the inquiries and projects it brings.

If each mix collected $100, a mixing-only model would need approximately 21–22 songs each working week over 48 weeks. At one to two hours each, that is roughly 21–43 hours of project work, plus any business time missing from those estimates. This is one scenario, not your verified current workload. We need to choose a combination of volume, fees, service mix, and schedule that can work.

You can handle business tasks yourself or arrange support for suitable tasks. That support needs a willing person, clear responsibilities, and a cost the business can carry. If the model cannot fund your income within the time you want to work, the economics, costs, service mix, or goal must change. Leaving those decisions informal leaves you without a dependable plan.

This process will help you make those choices, practice difficult conversations, and build a small routine you can maintain.

## Make uncomfortable changes manageable

### Pricing

Document inclusions and quote consistently, then examine actual effort, customer evidence, and business requirements. If the numbers show a shortfall, assess price, scope, delivery time, service mix, and costs. A rate increase may be necessary when alternatives cannot support the goal; discomfort does not resolve the shortfall.

Rehearse a straightforward explanation, using placeholders until terms are agreed:

> For this song, the fee is [amount]. That includes [work], [deliverables], and [revision process]. If the brief changes, I'll confirm any additional cost before doing the extra work.

For a budget mismatch, discuss a smaller scope only if it still helps the artist and is viable to deliver. Otherwise, explain the mismatch clearly. Deliberate discounted/community work should have a bounded capacity funded by the wider business model.

### Marketing

Choose activities Chase can explain comfortably: a permitted excerpt with his exact credit, a short explanation of a mixing decision, a relevant conversation about an existing client's next release, or a useful referral relationship. Evaluate methods by paid outcomes and effort.

Prepare examples and rehearse the language. A large generic content calendar does not address reluctance. Acquisition remains necessary; the methods and who performs them can change. No outreach or publication is authorized by this document.

### Boundaries and administration

Use one quote structure, one feedback method, a preparation checklist, and visible payment/project stages. Practice conversations about additional guests, new recordings, and extra revisions before they arise. Templates reduce repeated negotiation; the boundaries still need to be upheld.

## Initial working agreement to discuss

For an initial 30-day period, propose that Chase:

1. Uses the agreed scope/quote format for new paid work and records exceptions.
2. Records collected value and actual effort for completed jobs as they happen.
3. Names who handles each inquiry and its next action.
4. Schedules a manageable acquisition activity or assigns willing, affordable support.
5. Attends a weekly review and uses the evidence to make a specific operating decision.

The project lead supplies templates, rehearsal, early review support, and clearly stated choices. Agree activity volume with Chase; these are proposed responsibilities, not commitments he has already accepted.

At review, distinguish weak demand, an unsuitable offer, an impractical process, and actions that were not carried out. If implementation fails, simplify or reassign it and retest. Repeatedly planning around work no one will do is not a credible operating model.

## Choices if the required work cannot be sustained

Evaluate four routes using their actual economics: Chase performs a limited repeatable set of business responsibilities with support; he assigns suitable tasks to affordable help; he changes service mix, scope, studio costs, or capacity; or he revises the income/schedule/full-time objective.

These decisions belong to Chase. State when an option fails to support his goal. Do not recommend a partnership, spending, or premises change before examining the implications.

## Implications for the engagement

Include this assessment and owner working session before finalizing the commercial brief. Continue useful research, wireframes, and factual website repairs alongside it. Establish ownership of quotes, inquiry response, delivery, and ongoing updates before presenting the redesigned site as an implemented sales process.

Judge business evidence and maintained practices alongside design quality. Record recommendations, choices, implementation, outcomes, and unresolved dependencies. The evaluation must identify when business functions remain unassigned or the chosen model does not support the owner's stated goal.
